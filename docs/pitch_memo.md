# Indian Energy Exchange (NSE: IEX): Short

**Recommendation:** SHORT / SELL
**Current price:** ₹113.90 (confirmed 13-Sep-2026 live quote; the stock has moved 5-10% intraday on regulatory headlines repeatedly this year, verify again if presenting later)
**Base case target:** ₹58 (-49%)
**Bear case target:** ₹31 (-73%)
**Risk to the thesis (bull case):** ₹147 (+29%)
**Catalyst:** Supreme Court ruling on IEX's challenge to market coupling; CERC's final notification following Grid India's technical review. Neither has a confirmed date.

*Educational exercise built for interview preparation. Not investment research, not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. No non-public information was used, every figure below is built from public disclosures and press coverage of the regulatory process, current as of September 2026.*

---

## The thesis in three sentences

CERC's market coupling mandate will centralise electricity price discovery across every power exchange in India, and the direction of that policy is settled; only its timing is contested. IEX's 85-90% Day-Ahead Market share exists because liquidity begets liquidity; once every exchange shows the same price, that advantage stops mattering. At ₹113.90, the market is pricing in something close to the bull case where a residual customer relationship fully offsets the change; this memo argues that's the less likely outcome, and the base case is closer to ₹58.

## What IEX actually does

IEX runs India's dominant platform for trading electricity a day ahead of delivery, generators and buyers submit bids, IEX matches them and sets the clearing price. It is a financial-market-infrastructure business: high margins (78% net margin, TTM), asset-light, monopoly-like economics. That last part is exactly what's under threat.

## The catalyst

CERC's "market coupling" reform pools bids from every exchange (IEX, PXIL, HPX) into one centralised system that sets a single, uniform price across all of them. The stock has already priced this in once: it fell ~30% in a single session when the order was first confirmed in mid-2025, and has continued falling in steps as each subsequent legal and regulatory milestone has gone against IEX:

- **Feb 2026:** the appellate tribunal (APTEL) dismissed IEX's initial challenge
- **Apr 2026:** CERC issued draft implementing regulations; notably, **Grid India itself**, the body CERC has designated to run the new system, raised serious technical objections to the draft (software not "industry grade," untested at scale, no clarity on cost allocation)
- **Ongoing:** IEX has escalated to the **Supreme Court**, which has agreed to hear the case

The direction of travel has not reversed at any of these steps. The open question is purely how long implementation takes, not whether it happens.

## Why the market is still too optimistic

At ₹113.90 and a TTM EPS of roughly ₹5.27, IEX trades near 21.6x trailing earnings, a real, sourced comparable-company check on the `Comps` tab shows this is already a 62% discount to the ~57x median at which intact-moat Indian market infrastructure businesses (MCX, CDSL, CAMS) trade, consistent with the market already pricing in meaningful regulatory risk. Run that same multiple forward against FY28 earnings under each scenario in this model, and the current price only really clears in the **bull case**, where IEX's market share settles near 70% (from ~87% today) because more than 70% of trading volume already flows through API-based "tight coupling" with individual members, a switching-cost argument management has drawn explicitly from the NSE-vs-BSE precedent.

That argument is plausible. It is also **untested**, nobody has observed customer behaviour under an actual uniform-pricing regime yet, because it hasn't launched. The base case in this model (57% share, 25% fee compression) is not a doom scenario; it is simply "meaningful, not total, erosion," and it implies **47% downside** from here. You do not need to believe IEX's business is destroyed to be short this stock. You only need to believe the bull case's specific, unproven assumption is more likely to be partially right than fully right.

## Where most of the downside actually comes from

Decomposing the base case: **-39 percentage points of the -49% return comes purely from lower earnings**, holding today's ~21.6x multiple constant. Only the remaining **-17 points come from the market also re-rating the multiple down** to a more cautious 18x. This matters for the pitch: the thesis does not depend on assuming the market panics or misprices the stock further than it already has. It only requires the earnings decline itself to show up, and the price falls mechanically even at an unchanged multiple. The multiple compression is a secondary, conservative assumption on top of an already-sufficient earnings case.

## The bull case, stated fairly

IEX's own management has a real argument: over 70% of I-DAM cleared volume already flows through IEX's own bidding APIs, integrated deeply enough into members' own trading systems that switching has genuine friction even once prices are identical everywhere. Grid India's own objections to the draft rules, plus the Supreme Court referral, both raise the odds that implementation slips by a year or more; every quarter of delay is a quarter where IEX keeps earning at close to today's rate. If both hold, the ~+29% bull case in this model is achievable, and if the market went further and re-rated IEX all the way to the peer median (see `Comps` tab), the true upside risk to being short would be larger still.

**What would make this thesis wrong:** a Supreme Court ruling in IEX's favour, a materially watered-down final CERC framework (exempting certain contract types or segments from coupling), or early evidence, once any pilot or phased rollout begins, that customers are in fact staying on IEX despite uniform pricing.

## Risks to being short

The stock has already fallen 25-35% from its highs; a short here is not first-mover positioning, it's a bet the move isn't over. Regulatory processes in India can also stall for years, the original coupling directive dates to July 2025 and still isn't implemented, and the Grid India objections plus a Supreme Court referral both cut toward *more* delay, which the bull case benefits from directly. Position sizing and time horizon matter here more than in a typical long thesis, because the catalyst has no confirmed date.

## What I'd want to know next

The actual API/tight-coupling switching-cost data once any pilot phase launches; the Supreme Court's expected hearing timeline; and whether any other jurisdiction that has implemented similar market coupling (Europe's multi-NEMO model is the closest precedent) saw its dominant exchange retain share the way IEX's bull case requires.
